How EvoSim works

This page is the whole system in one read. Each section links to the guide that explains that part in depth.

The idea

An animal's behaviour is not coded: it is described by genes, short English sentences such as "Eat whenever you see grass." or "Run from any predator you see.". At each decision, the animal's senses describe its situation in a few words ("Food: 1-4 meters away. Predator: none within 20 meters."). A language model, the brain, reads the genes and the situation and gives a probability for each action the species can take: eat, flee, hide, follow, rest, mate, hunt. The animal draws one action from those probabilities and carries it out for a few ticks.

Animals that eat well and avoid predators live longer and breed more. A baby takes each gene from one parent or the other, and sometimes a gene mutates: a second language model, the mutator, rewrites the sentence. Sentences that help animals survive spread through the population; the others die out. That is evolution, with English as the genetic code.

The pieces

flowchart LR
    W[World] --> S1[Species: prey]
    W --> S2[Species: predator]
    W --> P[Tick phases]
    W --> SV[Services: ground, food, cover, brains, recorder]
    S1 --> M[Modules: senses, actions, genes, stats, rules, mutation]
    S1 --> A[(Animals: plain data)]
  • The World is a GameObject. Everything else is a component under it, so a scene is a complete experiment. (The world and its modules)
  • A species is a GameObject under the World. Its child components are its modules: senses, actions, genes, stats, life rules and mutation operators. Add a component and the species has that feature.
  • Animals are plain C# objects owned by their species, not GameObjects. Each has a position, stats such as energy and stamina, a genome, an age and a current action. (Animals, stats and death)
  • Services are shared parts under the World: the ground, food layers, cover, carcasses and eggs, the brains, the mutator, the recorder. (The environment)
  • Tick phases are components under the World's Phases object. The World runs them in hierarchy order, once per tick. (A tick, phase by phase)

One tick

Simulation time advances in ticks. A tick runs every phase in order:

Phase What happens
Sense each animal that decides this tick reads its senses: one token per sense, then its situation text
Ask brains situations already answered come from the memo or the cache; the others go to the brains, one batch per brain
Choose actions the World waits for the answers, then draws each deciding animal's action from its probabilities
Act each action gives an intent (go there, flee from that); the locomotion moves the animal; interactions happen on arrival (graze, strike, scavenge); living costs energy
Breed an animal that chose to mate and reaches a ready partner conceives a litter: one egg per baby, genes crossed over, mutations drawn and sent to the mutator
Hatch eggs due this tick hatch, once their mutated genes are back
Deaths killed animals die; every animal ages by one tick; death rules run (starvation, old age)
Migration species above their cap lose animals chosen at random
Environment food regrows, carcasses rot
Floor species below their floor get newcomers
Record a statistics row every 100 ticks, and the files are flushed; events are written as they happen

An animal does not decide every tick. In the reference scenes it decides every 4 ticks (the World's decision period), and each species' decision schedule staggers its animals by id, so about a quarter of them decide on each tick and the brain's work is spread out. A newborn decides at once; a busy animal (digesting a kill) does not decide. Between decisions an animal keeps its action. (Decisions and brains)

From senses to an action

flowchart LR
    SE[Senses] -->|tokens| SI[Situation text]
    G[Genes] --> Q[Query]
    SI --> Q
    Q --> MC{Memo or cache?}
    MC -->|known| PB[Probabilities]
    MC -->|new| BR[Brain reads the prompt] --> PB
    PB -->|draw with temperature| AC[Action]
  1. Each sense returns one token from a fixed list, for example here, adjacent, close, medium, far or none for the nearest food. The tokens together are the animal's observation. (Senses and prompts)
  2. The genes and the situation make a query. The brain turns it into a prompt from the species' template: a header, one line per action, the rules of the world, the animal's genes, the situation text, and the question.
  3. The same genes in the same situation always get the same answer from the same brain. The memo remembers answers during a run and the answer cache on disk across runs. Only new questions reach the model.
  4. The brain returns one probability per action. JEV, a language model served on localhost:8000, is the reference brain. The random brain answers uniformly and needs no server.
  5. The animal draws its action, after the probabilities are sharpened or flattened by the sampling temperature.

From an action to a movement

An action never moves the animal itself. It tells an ActContext where to go (walk to that grass, run away from that predator) and what to do on arrival (graze it, strike it). The species' locomotion then moves the animal as far as its speed and stamina allow, and the interaction happens if the target is within reach. (Actions and movement, The food web)

From parents to babies

A mating pair produces a litter. Each baby takes each gene from one parent or the other (crossover). Each gene then mutates with a small probability (3 % per gene per birth in the reference). A mutation asks the mutator model to rewrite the sentence, for example by making it stronger, weaker or about something else. Each baby starts as an egg that carries its genes until the mutator's answers are back. In the reference the incubation is 0 ticks: the eggs hatch in the same tick, and the tick waits for the mutator if it must. Give a species an incubation of a few ticks and the world goes on while the mutator works. (Genes and mutation, Reproduction and population)

Same seed, same run

Every random draw comes from a named stream derived from the World's seed. As long as the brain answers the same way, which the memo and the answer cache ensure, a run repeats exactly on the same machine: in the editor, headless, or in a player build, whatever the speed and the wait mode. Each run ends with an events hash that identifies it. (Determinism and replay)

What you watch and what you keep

The views only display: bodies follow their animals, with a marker coloured by their action, over a ground painted with food and cover. The recorder writes every event and statistic to Logs/EvoSim/runs/. (Views and real time, Recording and outputs)
