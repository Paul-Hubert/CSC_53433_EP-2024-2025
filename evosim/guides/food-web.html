The food web

Who eats whom is data, not code (04). Two components decide it together: the eaten thing carries an Edible, which says how much energy it gives, and the eater's Diet lists what it eats. A meal happens only if both sides allow it (SPEC-13). From the diets the World derives each species' prey and threats, which senses and actions use to find whom to hunt and whom to flee. Any number of species can share a world, and adding one changes no code.

How it works

  1. Edible. A resource layer, a species or an entity kind (eggs) becomes edible when its GameObject carries an Edible component. Without one, nothing can eat it, whatever the diets say (SPEC-10).
  2. Diet. Each species that eats has one Diet. Its entries name what it eats, each with an energy scale (SPEC-15). A species with no diet eats nothing.
  3. Resolution. When the world initializes, each diet resolves its entries by name into EdibleTargets (ARCH-08). Then the World derives the food web: a species whose diet strikes another is that species' threat, and the other is its prey (SPEC-12).
  4. Use. Senses and actions look for "threats", "prey" or "kin" through an AnimalSet. When an interaction applies, the resolver asks the eater's diet again whether this target may be eaten.

Diet entries

Entry Eats How Energy of one meal
a layer's name, e.g. Grass the layer's food items grazed: the item is consumed the layer's Edible Energy (25 for grass)
a species' id or display name, e.g. prey that species' living animals struck: killed with the hunter's kill chance the species' Edible Energy (60 for the prey)
carcass:<species>, e.g. carcass:prey carcasses that species leaves scavenged: one portion at a time the species' Edible Carcass Energy (30)
egg:<species> that species' eggs, while they incubate eaten whole the egg system's Edible Energy

Each entry has an Energy Scale (default 1) that multiplies the energy of that food for this eater. A diet that strikes a species also declares the hunter's kill.chance trait; its default is the diet's Kill Chance (0.5). For the steps to make something edible or change a diet, see Food, cover, entities and ground.

Where the Edible goes

What is eaten The Edible sits on
a layer's items the layer's GameObject (Environment/Grass in the reference scenes)
a species' animals the species' own GameObject, next to its Species component
a species' carcasses the same Edible as its animals: Carcass Portions above 0 make it leave carcasses; the world also needs a CarcassSystem
a species' eggs the EggSystem's GameObject (Environment/Eggs)

A disabled Edible counts as absent when the world starts (ARCH-06).

What a meal gives

The energy of a meal is the Edible's energy times the entry's Energy Scale. The diet's EnergyFrom can then adjust it for the eater (the reference returns it as it is), and Energy adds it, never above the maximum (ANIM-22). Every meal counts in the animal's Meals and its species' meal counter. After a kill or a carcass portion, a species with a Digestion module is busy digesting; grazing and eggs never make an animal busy.

Threats, prey and kin

The World builds the relations from the diets, in species order, when it initializes and again whenever a species is added during a run. They are never configured twice (SPEC-12).

Set Means Used by (reference)
Threats species whose diet strikes this one the prey's "Predator" sense and its flee action
Prey species this one's diet strikes the predator's "Prey" sense and its hunt action
Kin this species the kin senses ("Animal", "Other predator"), follow and mate
Listed species named on the module, by id or display name a flee from wolves only, for example

ThreatsOf(Species) and PreyOf(Species) return these lists. An animal standing in cover is hidden from its threats: their senses don't report it, their actions can't target it, and they never strike it (ENV-11). Its kin and its own prey still see it.

Carcasses

When a strike kills an animal whose Edible has Carcass Portions above 0, a carcass appears where it died (reference: 2 portions of 30 energy). The killer has already eaten: it may not eat from its own kill. Any other animal whose diet lists carcass:<species> may eat one portion each. The carcass is gone when its portions are eaten, or when it rots after Carcass Ticks (100); rotting happens in the environment phase.

The reference hunt action runs to the nearer of the nearest prey and the nearest carcass it may eat, and a carcass wins a tie, being a sure meal. A diet that eats carcasses adds the carcass system's line to its species' prompt: "A kill leaves a carcass that up to 2 other predators can eat from."

Cannibals

A species may list itself in its diet (SPEC-11). It is then among its own prey and its own threats: its hunt action chases its own kind, and its flee action runs from it, like from any threat. An animal never targets itself.

Species added during a run

A species cloned from another during a run (SPEC-30) inherits its relations in both directions (SPEC-31): it eats what its parent species eats, and the hunters of the parent hunt it too, through its own Edible. The World rebuilds the food web when the species is added (RebuildFoodWeb()).

In the editor

  • Food Web window (Window ▸ EvoSim ▸ Food Web): species and layers as a graph. Green edges graze, red strike, brown scavenge carcasses, yellow eat eggs. In Edit mode press Build the food web, which validates the world to resolve the diets. Below the graph: the derived threats of each species and the food-web messages.
  • Species inspector: Show the computed tables lists what the species eats with the energy of each, its own Edible, and its derived threats.
  • Validation reports a diet entry that names nothing (V-20), a flee, hunt or sense with nobody to look for (V-21), a species with no food (V-22), a diet target with no Edible (V-24, with a fix), and an Edible no diet lists (V-25).
