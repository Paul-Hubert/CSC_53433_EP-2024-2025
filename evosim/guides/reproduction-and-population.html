Reproduction and population

Evolution needs births: an animal that eats well, escapes predators and finds a partner leaves babies, and its genes spread. This page follows a birth from the mate decision to the newborn, then explains the limits that keep each population in bounds: a cap above, a floor below. What happens to the genes on the way, mutation in particular, is in Genes and mutation.

How a birth happens

flowchart LR
    D[Decision: mate] --> B{Breed phase: ready partner within reach?}
    B -->|no| N[No litter]
    B -->|yes| L[Litter size; parents pay]
    L --> E[One egg per baby: crossover, mutations drawn]
    E --> H{Hatch phase: due and genome complete?}
    H -->|yes| A[Babies join the species]
    A --> M[Migration: back to the cap]
    M --> F[Floor: newcomers if too few]

Mating

An animal whose brain chose mate walks toward the nearest ready partner it sees. The breeding itself happens in the breed phase, after every animal has acted (TICK-06):

  1. The phase takes, in id order, each animal whose current action is mate, that is ready and hasn't bred in this decision period. Ready means alive, adult (age at least the Maturity trait, 150 ticks) and with at least Mate Energy (50 points) (IsReady(Animal), REPRO-01).
  2. It pairs it with the nearest ready animal of its species within Reach (1 m) that hasn't bred either; ties go to the lower id. One partner's choice is enough: the partner may be resting or eating (REPRO-02).
  3. Both are marked as having bred. Each animal breeds at most once per decision period: the mark is cleared at its next decision (REPRO-03).

With Sexual off on the MatingRule, or the World's Asexual control (C7) on, a ready animal that chose mate reproduces alone and pays the whole cost (REPRO-04). Killed animals never breed (REPRO-05).

Litters and their cost

The Litter decides how many babies a mating makes (REPRO-10):

  1. k is drawn uniformly in [Min, Max] = [2, 4], from the species' litter stream.
  2. Each baby costs Child Energy (40 points), shared equally by the parents: with two parents, 20 each per baby.
  3. k is cut so that no parent pays more energy than it has, but never below Min. A poor parent can be left with zero energy or less, and starves at the death check. This is a choice of the contract, not an accident.

Each parent then pays its share × k, its offspring counter grows by k, and it is marked as having bred (REPRO-11). With the reference values, a parent at 50 energy pays for 2 babies (40 points) and keeps 10; at 100 it can pay for 4.

Crossover and eggs

Each baby gets its own crossover, so siblings usually differ (GENE-32). The reference UniformCrossover takes each locus from one parent or the other with probability ½, independently, from the species' mutation stream (GENE-30). A baby of a single parent copies its genome (GENE-31). The baby's mutations are then drawn, locus by locus (MUT-30).

Each baby becomes an Egg, laid at the first parent's position in the World's EggSystem (REPRO-20). An egg holds the parents, the generation (the larger parent generation + 1), the alleles after crossover, the mutations still running, the conception tick and the hatch tick: conception + the species' Incubation Ticks. Eggs are not animals: they don't sense, decide, move, eat, breed or count toward caps (REPRO-22). In the reference nothing eats them; a species can be given a taste for eggs (REPRO-23, see Reproduction and population).

Hatching

The hatch phase runs right after the breed phase. Eggs due at this tick hatch in conception order (REPRO-22), but only once their genome is complete: if the mutator's answers aren't in, the tick waits (MUT-32, REPRO-24). With the reference incubation of 0 ticks, babies hatch in the tick they were conceived, as in the prototype (REPRO-21).

Each baby starts with the litter's Child Energy, full stamina, age 0, the heading drawn at conception, at the egg's place (REPRO-12). It has no action until its first decision, at the start of the next tick (REPRO-13). A birth event records its parents, generation, genome, litter size and mutations.

Caps and migration

Each species has a CapRule with a Cap and a Mode (POP-01):

Mode At the cap
Migrate (reference) Births go on. In the migration phase, if the species is above its cap, animals chosen uniformly at random leave the world (death cause migrated) until it is back at the cap. Animals born this tick are spared, unless too few older animals remain.
Block No conception while the species is at or above its cap; a litter is cut to the places left, possibly to zero.

Migration draws from the species' own migration stream and ignores genes, stats and position, so it favours no gene (POP-02). Migrate was chosen over Block because blocked births led to boom-and-bust crashes (11 §4).

Floors and newcomers

Each species has a FloorRule. In the floor phase, the last but one phase of the tick, while a species has fewer living animals than its Floor, a newcomer arrives (POP-03): a founder genome drawn from the founder pools, a random walkable position, the starting stats, generation 0, recorded as an immigrant event. It decides from the next tick (TICK-05). At tick 0 each species gets its Initial Population of founders the same way, recorded as founder events (POP-04).

Newcomers bring founder genes back and restart that species' evolution, so a run that needs them is reported (POP-05). The aim is a world that never needs them: litters of 2 to 4 and migration at the cap made both reference species last without newcomers.

The main types

Type Role
MatingRule who is adult and ready, the breeding reach, one or two parents
Litter the litter size and the energy each baby costs
Crossover, UniformCrossover how the parents' genomes mix
Incubation ticks from conception to hatching
Egg, EggSystem a conceived baby; the world's eggs, laid and hatched in order
CapRule, FloorRule the population's upper and lower limits
BreedPhase, HatchPhase the phases that conceive and hatch
MigrationPhase, FloorPhase the phases that apply the cap and the floor

In the editor

  • The species inspector's Population foldout shows the initial population, the floor, the cap and its mode, and the living count in Play mode.
  • In Play mode, the Scene view's EvoSim overlay draws a yellow circle under each egg (the Eggs toggle).
  • Window ▸ EvoSim ▸ Event Log lists birth, immigrant, egg_lost and death events as they happen; filter by kind, species or animal, and follow one animal's descendants.
  • Window ▸ EvoSim ▸ Ecology Monitor graphs the populations and counts births, founders and deaths by cause.
  • Validation warns about a breeding species without an EggSystem (V-20), without a Litter, a crossover or an Energy module (V-26), a floor or initial population above the cap (V-32), a bad litter range (V-33), and a smallest litter that costs more than the mate energy (V-34).
