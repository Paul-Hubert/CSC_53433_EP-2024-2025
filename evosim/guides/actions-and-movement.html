Actions and movement

The brain only picks an action. The action then does the work, every tick until the animal's next decision: it looks for its target, says where the animal wants to go and what to do on arrival. The species' locomotion moves the animal, an interaction (graze, strike, scavenge) happens if the target is within reach, and the metabolism charges the tick. This split keeps actions short and readable, and lets each species move in its own way (07).

One animal's act step

The act phase (ActPhase) runs these steps for every living animal, in the act order:

flowchart TD
    B{Busy, or no action?} -->|yes| ST[Stay, no interaction]
    B -->|no| P[The action plans: an intent and an interaction]
    P --> M[The locomotion moves it: speed, stamina, ground]
    M --> R{Within reach, and the diet allows it?}
    R -->|yes| I[The interaction applies]
    R -->|no| S
    I --> S[Metabolism and stamina settle the tick]
    ST --> S
    S --> C[Busy countdown]
  1. Plan. The animal's action receives an ActContext and calls its methods. Nothing moves yet.
  2. Move. The species' Locomotion turns the intent into a movement and reports the meters moved.
  3. Interact. The InteractionResolver applies the planned interaction if the target is within reach, still there, and allowed by the eater's diet.
  4. Settle. Metabolism charges energy for what the animal did, and Stamina pays the meters or recovers. A busy animal's countdown drops by one.

Planning with the act context

An action never moves an animal and never changes another one (ACT-06). It only asks questions and states a plan:

Kind Methods What they do
Queries NearestAnimal, NearestReadyPartner, NearestResource, NearestCarcass, NearestEgg, NearestEntity<T>, NearestCover, InCover, IsReady, Vision Return only what the animal's senses could report: within its vision (20 m in the reference), never itself, never a killed animal, never an animal hidden from it in cover (ACT-05). null means nothing in sight.
Intents WalkTo, RunTo (a point or an animal, with a stop distance), RunAwayFrom, WalkAwayFrom, Stay, Wander, Search Say where to go this tick. Running uses the species' running speed.
Arrival OnArrival(Interaction), OnArrival(IInteraction) Say what to do once within reach: graze, strike, scavenge, eat an egg, or a custom effect.

Before each action runs, the context is reset to stay, no interaction. The intent methods replace each other: the last call wins. The same goes for OnArrival. An action that calls nothing makes the animal stay.

WalkTo and RunTo stop at the given distance and never overshoot it (MOVE-04). An animal already within that distance stays. The action looks its target up again every tick, so it follows a moving target (ACT-02).

The reference actions

Action Class Target Intent On arrival Searches when
eat EatAction nearest food item of a layer its diet grazes walk to it (stop 0); stay if already on it graze, on the tick of arrival too no food in sight
flee FleeAction nearest threat run straight away from it — no threat in sight
hide HideAction nearest cover walk (or run) into it; stay once inside — no cover in sight, or no cover module
follow FollowAction nearest animal of its own species walk toward it, stop at 1 m — no kin in sight
rest RestAction — stay — never
mate MateAction nearest ready partner walk toward it, stop at 1 m — (the breed phase breeds it) no ready partner in sight
hunt HuntAction the nearer of the nearest prey and the nearest carcass it may eat; a carcass wins a tie run toward it, stop at 1 m strike the prey, or scavenge the carcass neither in sight

The prey has eat, flee, hide, follow, rest and mate; the predator has hunt, follow, rest and mate (04 §5). Flee has an option from the prototype, Flee Into Cover (off in the reference): an animal already in cover stays, and cover within Cover Seek meters (6) is run into instead of running away. Mating itself is not an interaction: the mate action only brings the animal next to a partner, and the breed phase does the rest (ACT-13).

Searching

When an action has nothing to act on, it calls Search() (ACT-04). The animal wanders: it keeps its heading, and each tick turns by ±45° or ±90° with probability Wander Turn P (0.25 on the locomotion). Blocked by a border or an obstacle, it picks a new random heading. It walks.

The first search after a decision sets the animal's Searching flag and counts one invalid choice for its species (the prototype's name). The next decision clears the flag. The <species>_invalid column of stats.csv counts them.

Busy animals

A busy animal (BusyTicks above 0) doesn't decide and doesn't move: the act phase gives it a stay plan with no interaction. It pays the rest cost and recovers stamina. The counter drops by one at the end of each tick that started busy. When it reaches 0, the animal has no action and decides at the start of the next tick (ANIM-30). In the reference only digestion makes animals busy: see Animals, stats and death.

Moving

Each species has exactly one locomotion component (MOVE-01, V-13). The reference KinematicLocomotion moves in straight lines, without physics (MOVE-04):

  1. The distance is the smallest of the speed (the animal's speed.walk or speed.run trait), its stamina budget and the intent's maximum distance. A stop distance is therefore never overshot.
  2. SlideTo(Vector3, Vector3) moves along that line as far as walkable ground allows. Blocked by a border or an obstacle, the animal slides along it.
  3. If that made no progress, SideStep(Vector3, float, RandomStream) takes a step of the same length in a random direction, drawn from the species' actions stream.
  4. The locomotion sets the position and returns the meters moved (MOVE-06).

The stamina budget is the animal's stamina in meters (ANIM-21): an exhausted animal can't move, whatever it chose. A species without a stamina module has no limit. Whatever the locomotion does is the tick's result (MOVE-01); after it, the act phase turns the animal toward where it moved (a wandering animal keeps its own heading). If a locomotion ever leaves an animal on non-walkable ground, the act phase puts it back and logs a warning (MOVE-02).

Speeds are traits, so number genes can change them (MOVE-03). Reference: prey walk and run 1 m per tick, predators walk 1 and run 2. A predator outruns a prey over short chases; the prey has twice the stamina (07 §4).

Interactions and reach

After moving, in the same tick, the planned interaction applies if the target is within reach (ACT-10) and both sides allow it: the target has an Edible component and the eater's Diet lists it (SPEC-13). See The food web.

Interaction Built with Reach (reference) Effect
graze Interaction.Graze(item) 0.5 m from the item the item is consumed (an item goes to the first animal that reaches it); the eater gains its energy
strike Interaction.Strike(animal) 1 m with the hunter's kill chance, the prey is killed; the hunter eats it and digests; a carcass may appear
scavenge Interaction.Scavenge(carcass) 1 m one portion is eaten; the eater gains its energy and digests
eat egg Interaction.EatEgg(egg) 0.5 m the egg is eaten and never hatches (REPRO-23)
custom OnArrival(IInteraction) its own Reach, from the point the intent was heading to whatever its Apply does

Kills. A hunter strikes at most once per tick (ACT-12); a miss uses that strike too. The chance that a strike kills is the hunter's kill.chance trait (Kill Chance on its diet, 0.5 in the reference), drawn from the hunter species' actions stream. An animal standing in cover that hides it from the hunter is never struck (ENV-11). A killed animal is marked at once: for the rest of the tick it is not sensed, not targeted, doesn't act and doesn't breed. It is removed in the death phase (ANIM-41). A prey that survives a strike can be struck again by another hunter in the same tick.

Act order

The act order is the Order setting of the act phase (ACT-30). The random orders come from the act-order stream, so a seed repeats them (ACT-32).

Order How animals take turns
Species In Turn (reference) species in the World's order; within a species, a fresh random order each tick. Each animal plans, moves, interacts and settles before the next one starts, so prey move before predators strike.
All Mixed every animal of every species in one fresh random order each tick, one after another.
Simultaneous every action plans from the state at the start of the phase; each locomotion moves its animals as a group; interactions apply in a fresh random order (a contested item goes to the first one drawn); then every animal that was not killed settles.

In the two sequential orders an animal acts on the state left by those before it: positions, eaten items, kills (ACT-31). Reference numbers are measured with Species In Turn (ACT-33).

After the move: the metabolism

Each living animal then pays for its tick, once (ANIM-20): a base cost plus a cost per meter moved, or a lower rest cost when it stood still resting or busy, plus any extra cost its locomotion reports (slopes, mud). Then its stamina pays the meters moved, or recovers. The numbers are in Animals, stats and death.

In the editor

  • Scene view, in Play mode: select an animal to see its vision, a line to its target and a circle coloured by its action. The EvoSim overlay can write each animal's action letter above it.
  • Animal Inspector (Window ▸ EvoSim ▸ Animal Inspector): the selected animal's current action and target, its busy reason, its stats.
