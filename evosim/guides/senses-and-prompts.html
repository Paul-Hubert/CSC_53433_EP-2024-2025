Senses and prompts

A brain never sees the world. It reads a few words about it. At each decision, every sense of the animal's species turns one aspect of the world (the nearest food, the energy level) into one token from a short, fixed list. The tokens together are the animal's observation, and the species writes them as a situation text such as "Energy: low. Food: 1-4 meters away.". The species then puts that text into its prompt, with the rules of the world and the animal's genes. Discrete tokens keep the prompt short and let identical situations be answered once (06 — Senses).

How it works

flowchart LR
    S[Senses] -->|one token each| O[Observation]
    O -->|Write per token| T[Situation text]
    P[Prompt template] --> F[Full prompt]
    G[Genes block] --> F
    T --> F
    B[Brain's answer instruction] --> F
  1. The sense phase picks the animals that decide this tick (Decisions and brains).
  2. It casts the rays of the batched senses, then calls every sense of every deciding animal. Each sense returns an index into its token list (SENSE-01).
  3. The indices, in sense order, make an Observation (SENSE-02). Describe(Observation, TextStyle) has each sense write its token and joins the fragments with spaces: the situation text (SENSE-10).
  4. A brain that reads text fills the species' prompt template, built when the run started, with the animal's genes, the situation text and its own answer instruction (Prompt).

Senses and tokens

Type Role
Sense reads one animal and returns one token from its fixed Tokens list
BandedSense a sense of distance, in bands: "here", "adjacent" … "far", "none"
BatchedSense a sense that casts rays, all in one batch per tick
SenseContext what a sense may read: the state at the start of the tick, through the same queries as actions
Observation one token index per sense, in sense order
ObservationView an observation read by sense label, as actions do in IsRelevant

The senses' depth-first order under the species is the order of the tokens in the observation and of the fragments in the situation text. Each sense has a Label ("Food", "Predator"), which starts its V1 fragment; two senses of one species can't share a label (V-17). A sense sees the world as it was at the start of the tick (SENSE-04), and never an animal hidden from it in cover nor one killed this tick (SENSE-06).

The number of possible observations is the product of the token counts (SENSE-05). The species inspector shows it as Observation space. Validation warns above 100 000 (V-50), because the memo and the answer cache then rarely help.

Distance bands

A BandedSense turns a distance into a band (SENSE-20). Its Bands setting (Bands) holds the edges 1, 4 and 10 m and a vision of 20 m:

Token Distance V1 text
here (food, cover) within reach, or standing in it Food: here.
adjacent ≤ 1 m Food: within 1 meter.
close 1–4 m Food: 1-4 meters away.
medium 4–10 m Food: 4-10 meters away.
far 10–20 m Food: 10-20 meters away.
none nothing within the vision Food: none within 20 meters.

Vision is a trait, "vision", so a number gene can change it (SENSE-22). The sense reads each animal's own vision when it looks. The texts state the trait's default, so one token always gives one text. Band edges must be positive, increasing and below the vision (SENSE-21, V-30).

Situation texts: V1 and V2

The World's Text Style chooses how every sense writes (SENSE-11). V1 is terse, V2 is first person. The reference uses V1. The same prey situation in both styles:

V1: Energy: low. Stamina: high. Food: 1-4 meters away. Predator: 4-10 meters away. Cover: here.
    Animal: 10-20 meters away, ready to mate. Age: adult.

V2: I am hungry and weak. I am rested. The nearest food is 1-4 meters away. The nearest predator is 4-10 meters away.
    I am in cover. The nearest other animal is 10-20 meters away. It is ready to mate. I am an adult.

A text depends on the token only, never on the animal: the same observation always gives the same text (SENSE-10). The answer cache relies on it. Distances always say "meters" (SENSE-13). A level sense takes its V2 sentences from its Words field. A banded sense builds them from its Noun ("food", "other animal").

Batched raycasts

A sense that needs line of sight or the terrain ahead derives from BatchedSense. It asks for its rays in RequestRays(Animal, RayBatch). The sense phase casts the rays of every deciding animal in one batch (SENSE-30), then the sense reads its results through Rays. The tokens must be the same as when rays are cast one at a time (SENSE-31). Rays never hit the views, so what an animal senses never depends on rendering (RAND-11). No reference sense casts rays; the test sense ObstacleSense does.

Attachments

A sense may also attach an object to the query, such as a small camera image, for a brain that reads images (SENSE-40). A query with an attachment is memoised and cached only if the sense gives a key for it (SENSE-41). The species' brain must accept attachments (V-51). No reference brain does. See A sense.

How the prompt is written

When the run starts, Build(Species) writes each species' prompt template (Prompt), in this order (PROMPT-01):

Part Comes from
Header the species' Prompt Header ("You decide what a wild animal does next in a simple world.")
Actions: and one line per action, in action order each action's Description: - eat: go to the nearest visible food and eat it
Rule lines every species module's WritePromptRules (search rule, locomotion, stamina, mating), then every world service's (the carcass line)
Genes intro the species' Genes Intro ("This animal's instincts (its genes)…")
{genes} one line per gene the brain reads, in locus order: - eat: "Eat whenever food is close." (PROMPT-02)
Situation: {situation} the situation text
{ask} the brain's AnswerInstruction, hard-coded in the brain because its parsing depends on it (PROMPT-07)

The reference prey's prompt, for a founder in one situation, with the Ollama points brain (shortened):

You decide what a wild animal does next in a simple world.

Actions:
- eat: go to the nearest visible food and eat it
- flee: run away from the nearest predator
- hide: go to the nearest cover and stay in it; predators can't see or catch an animal in cover
…
Animals move 1 meter per step. Predators run 2 meters per step when they hunt,
but they have 30 stamina against an animal's 60, so a long chase tires them first.
…
This animal's instincts (its genes). They define its personality: follow them
even when they seem unwise. Instincts that are meaningless have no effect.
- eat: "Eat whenever food is close."
- flee: "Run from any predator you see."
…
Situation: Energy: low. Stamina: high. Food: 1-4 meters away. Predator: 4-10 meters away. Cover: 1-4 meters away. Animal: none within 20 meters. Age: adult.

Distribute 100 points across the actions according to how likely this animal is to choose each.

JEV adds no instruction: it sends this text without its last line, followed by its own question and the actions as lettered options. The template's Id is a hash of its text. It is part of the answer cache's keys, so editing a header, a description or a rule line asks the model again (PROMPT-05).

Placeholders

Numbers in descriptions and rule lines come from the modules' settings, so the prompt never contradicts the world (PROMPT-03). The speed line above is written Animals move {walkSpeed} meter per step. Predators run {predator.Locomotion.runSpeed} meters per step….

Placeholder Reads
{field} a field or property of the module writing the line: {walkSpeed}
{Module.field} another module of the same species, named by its class, a base class or its GameObject: {Stamina.max}
{species.Module.field} a module of another species: {predator.Stamina.max}
{name} this species' display name
{species.name}, {species.id} another species' display name or id
{{ and }} a literal brace

A field can be public or private. Numbers are written as the brain reads them ("4", "2.5"), booleans as "yes" or "no". An unknown placeholder stays in the text and validation reports it (V-53). The header and the genes intro have no writing module, so only {name} and the dotted forms work there.

Frozen prompts

A species can use a fixed text instead of the assembled one: set its Frozen Prompt to a text asset that contains {genes}, {situation} and {ask} (PROMPT-04). The prototype's prompts in 08 §7 are examples. Placeholders in it are still filled. A missing hole is reported (V-53).

In the editor

  • Select a species. In Edit mode, press Show the computed tables. The inspector lists the senses in situation-text order, with their tokens and the V1 and V2 texts of the first token. It also shows the Observation space and the Signature, which changes when a sense is added, removed, renamed or moved (V-12).
  • Its Prompt preview builds the exact text a brain receives. Pick a Brain, a Founder genome, a Text style and one token per sense (or Random situation), then press Build. It shows the estimated token count and the brain's limit; Copy puts the text on the clipboard.
  • Window ▸ EvoSim ▸ Animal Inspector shows the last situation text of the selected animal.
  • Window ▸ EvoSim ▸ Ask the Brain asks brains about a genome and a situation of your choice.
