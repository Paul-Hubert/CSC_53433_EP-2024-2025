Remarks

What the reference does, step by step (the exercise MyLlmMutation rewrites it):

  1. Initialize() reads the deck: one instruction per line, blank lines and lines starting with # skipped (Instructions(string), MUT-10).
  2. StartMutation(Gene, Allele, RandomStream) draws, for each of the tries tries in order, an instruction index (rng.Range(0, count)) and a seed (rng.NextUInt() & 0x7FFFFFFF): all of them at conception, even the tries a good first answer will make useless, so the stream never depends on the answers (MUT-30).
  3. It submits the parent sentence, these draws, the context line, the deck and the word limit (the smaller of its own and the gene's) to the World's MutatorService, which returns a running job.
  4. The service sends the first tries of the tick as one batch, cleans and checks each answer (Clean(string), Reject(string, string, int), MUT-12), and sends a rejected answer's next try in the next batch (MUT-13). When every try is rejected the job fails and the gene keeps its allele (MUT-04).
  5. Without a MutatorService, or with an empty deck, the job fails at once ("no mutator"), after the draws.

The mutator is blind (CORE-07): its prompt holds the context line, the instruction and the sentence, nothing about the animal or the world. A mutant's operator detail is llm#n, n being the instruction's index in the deck.
